## 2 · The benefit sentence computes

The sentence is one template with three shapes, chosen by the arithmetic — never the other way
round (`g` = the donor's gift, `s` = the relief span, `n` = the net-support span):

| shape | when | zh / en |
|---|---|---|
| `d.benefit.ok` | the gift is fully deductible | `您的 {g} 捐款可全數扣稅，享約 {s} 稅務扣減；只需約 {n} 實際支持，即可成就全額100%救盲力量。` |
| `d.benefit.capped` | the 35% cap bites | `您的 {g} 捐款的扣稅額受 35% 上限所限，享約 {s} 稅務扣減；…` |
| `d.benefit.zero` | income at or below the allowance | the existing "no salaries tax, the gift saves you nothing" line |

The capped shape exists because the uncapped one would be **false** for a HK$250,000 gift at
HK$500,000 income — only HK$175,000 is deductible there. The client's clause is kept intact where
it is true and swapped only where it is not; `qa/poc_test.py` asserts both directions.

`HK$182 – 221` and `HK$1,079 – 1,118` are the client's own rendering: one currency mark, one space,
an en dash, then the bare second figure. `qa/verify_six_examples.py` compares the whole HK$1,300
sentence to the sheet's string, character for character, in both languages.

The 35% line (`#cap`) follows the same rule: `2. …：此捐款額可全數扣除` only while the whole gift
really is deductible, else `2. …：可扣除 HK$175,000，其餘不能扣除`.
